What Business Goals Is Your Current Website Not Meeting?

A business website can miss four goals that directly affect revenue: appearing in AI-generated answers, converting visitors into customers, delivering a seamless experience for every visitor, and supporting social media marketing campaigns.

Each of these goals is influenced by decisions made during the planning, writing, and development stages. This makes website development a critical factor in determining whether a business achieves these goals or falls short.

A brief that clearly defines all four objectives gives the website development team measurable targets to design, write, and test against before launch.


Is your website development ready for AI answers?

It needs to be, because Google now places AI summaries above some results and people click fewer links when one appears. Pew Research Center analysed the browsing of 900 U.S. adults in March 2025 and found that they clicked a traditional search result in 8% of visits to pages with an AI summary, compared with 15% of visits to pages without one.

A page is easier to quote when it answers one question directly, so website development should give every page a question style heading, place the answer in the first two sentences, and follow it with details such as prices, locations and service names. Website development should also keep that information as plain text on the page, since search tools read text directly. No tactic guarantees that an AI tool will cite your site, yet a plain answer gives it something to quote and a slogan gives it nothing.


Does website development affect how much customers spend?

It can, because website development shapes page speed, and page speed influences how far visitors travel through a buying journey. A study commissioned by Google and conducted by 55 and Deloitte tracked 37 European and American brand sites and found that after a 0.1 second improvement in four mobile speed metrics, retail shoppers spent 9.2% more.

Website development teams can protect speed by setting a loading budget for every new feature, compressing images before upload, and removing plugins that no longer serve a purpose. Images, scripts and plugins all add loading time, so website development decisions made on day one affect speed long after launch. The team should also test on a mid-range phone using mobile data, not only on office wifi.


Can everyone use the website your website development team built?

Website development has to plan for accessibility, because problems are widespread even among the top one million home pages. WebAIM tested those pages in February 2026 and detected WCAG 2 failures on 95.9% of them. Low contrast text was the most common failure, which means a visitor with weak eyesight, or one holding a phone in bright sunlight, may struggle to read your offer.

Website development should include a check of text contrast, form labels and image descriptions before launch, because the WCAG standards cover all three. Contrast, labels and descriptions are decided while pages are designed, so website development is the natural moment to get them right.


Does your social media marketing lead to a website that matches it?

It should, because social media marketing works best when the page people land on continues the message that made them click. DataReportal counted 500 million social media user identities in India in October 2025, a pool of accounts that social media marketing can reach, and a link click from any of them can land on your website.

Social media marketing reports can show clicks, while the website shows what happens after the click, so the two sets of numbers deserve to be read side by side. Each social media marketing campaign therefore needs a landing page with the same offer and visuals as the post or ad, plus one clear next step.

Website development and social media marketing also work better together when the website team can see the content calendar and the social team can see the site analytics. Website development should also tag each social media marketing link so the analytics can show which campaign sent each visitor. Consider a reel that took three weeks to produce but links to a page still announcing last Diwali’s sale, where the viewer who clicks finds a message that no longer matches.


Is website development making your forms and checkout harder than needed?

It can, because website development shapes checkout and form design, which affect how many visitors finish a purchase or an enquiry. Baymard Institute tested the checkouts of large US and EU ecommerce sites and estimates that the average large site could gain a 35.26% increase in conversion rate through better checkout design. The same thinking applies to enquiry forms on service websites.

Website development for a service business can run the same test on a contact page, using a short form with one clear action, followed by a confirmation message once it is sent. A website development brief should count the steps and fields between interest and action, and each field should stay only if the business needs it to reply. Every extra field is extra effort that the visitor has to spend before getting an answer.


Where should website development planning start?

It should start with business goals, because pages and features should follow the outcomes the business wants. Write down what your website development must achieve this quarter, such as qualified enquiries, bookings or sales, and attach one number to each goal. Then check your website development against the gaps above: answers that AI tools can quote, pages that load quickly on a phone, forms and text that every visitor can use, and a landing page for each social media marketing campaign. A website development plan that begins with a colour palette skips the question of what the site is for.

Frequently Asked Questions

A website should support the goals that bring in revenue, such as qualified enquiries, bookings, sales and repeat visits. Attach one number to each goal, for example the count of form submissions per month, so you can tell whether the site is doing its job.

Compare the number of visitors with the number who complete the action you want, such as sending an enquiry or buying a product. Google Analytics can report this once you set up key events for actions like form submissions, and it lets you compare results on phones and computers.

AEO stands for answer engine optimization, which means writing pages so that search engines and AI tools can pull a clear answer straight from them. GEO stands for generative engine optimization, which applies the same idea to AI tools that write their own answers. Both matter because answers now appear on the results page itself, and a page with a direct answer under each heading is easier to quote.

A quarterly review is a practical rhythm, covering loading speed on a phone, form completion, broken links and whether each page still answers a real customer question. Review sooner after a redesign or a large campaign.
