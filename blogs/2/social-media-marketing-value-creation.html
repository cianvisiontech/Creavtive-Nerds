Empowering the Role of Social Media Marketing in Value Creation

Social media marketing creates value when it builds the memory, trust and demand that a business can trace to enquiries and sales, not when it collects likes. That happens when a clear brand strategy shapes every post, when content is designed for how people discover and share brands, and when results are judged against business outcomes.

What does value creation mean in social media marketing?

Value creation means that a stranger comes to recognise a brand, believe it and prefer it when a need arises. A like shows that someone saw a post, but it does not show that the brand will be remembered next month.

The signals that come closer to value are saved posts, links forwarded to friends, searches for the brand name, enquiries and repeat purchases, because each one shows a person acting on what they saw.

Social media marketing can influence each of these signals, which is why it belongs in business planning, not just the content calendar. It also changes how a team should report, because a monthly report that lists likes and followers describes activity, while a report that lists enquiries and brand searches describes progress.

Where do customers discover brands, and where does social media marketing fit?

Often on social platforms, but rarely through one channel alone. A Deloitte India report from September 2026 found that nearly 80 percent of Indian home shoppers discover products through social media. Yet DataReportal’s Digital 2026 report shows that even the three biggest discovery channels, namely search engines, TV ads and social media ads, can together introduce a brand to only 62.2 percent of the world’s online adults. Social media marketing therefore works best as one layer of discovery that supports search, word of mouth and offline presence, and not as a stand-alone growth engine.

Why does organic reach shrink as a page grows?

Platforms show each post to only part of an account’s audience, and that share tends to fall as the follower count rises. Socialinsider’s tracking of brand pages from January 2025 to August 2026 found that Facebook pages with 100,000 to 1 million followers reached an average of just 0.5 percent of them per post. The same analysis found that Reels did not deliver the reach advantage their reputation suggests. This is the uncomfortable side of social media marketing, because the platform’s algorithm, and not only the brand, shapes how far each post travels. The practical answer in social media marketing is to make content that people save and send to someone else, since shared content can travel beyond the follower list.

Does AI search use social media marketing content?

Yes, because AI tools cite social and community platforms among their sources. When Peec studied what AI platforms cite in the US in March 2026, it found Reddit and YouTube in the top five most cited sources on all five platforms it tracked, which were ChatGPT, Google AI Mode, Gemini, Perplexity and Google AI Overviews. A brand’s videos and answers in community conversations can therefore become visible in AI answers, which makes clear, plainly worded content useful for search as well as for social media marketing.

What is dark social and why does it hide results?

Dark social is content shared through private channels such as WhatsApp chats and direct messages, where analytics tools usually cannot tell where a visit came from. A recommendation sent to a friend can therefore bring a buyer without leaving a trace in platform numbers, and such visits often appear as direct traffic. Asking every new customer how they found the brand, adding a “how did you hear about us” field to forms, and watching direct visits to deep pages after a post goes live are simple ways to see what social media marketing achieves beyond the dashboard. Tagged links on share buttons can separate WhatsApp clicks from direct visits.

Why does brand strategy matter for social media?

Brand strategy defines who a company serves, what it stands for and why anyone should care, and it decides what each post is supposed to do. WARC’s Pace Principle 2.0, based on research in Asia, shows why that matters. It found that broad emotional campaigns delivered nearly four times the long-term effects of broad social campaigns. It also warns of a “social sprint loop”, in which brands create more content to chase virality and optimise for algorithms instead of building lasting brand memory.

WARC treats social as a creative strategy that gets people talking and sharing, while emotional work builds memory across a whole category, so social media marketing needs both. It also describes a platform ceiling, where content decays fast and growth stalls when paid media stops.

Brand strategy tells a team which job a post is doing, and a good test is whether a competitor could post the same content under its own logo. If it could, the brand strategy is not yet visible in the work.

How can brands create value with social media marketing?

Start by writing the brand strategy in one sentence that says who the brand serves and why it matters, then check every post against it. Give each post one job, such as teaching, entertaining or inspiring, and answer real customer questions from comments and sales calls in the first line of captions and scripts. Build content to be saved and sent, because shares carry a brand beyond its followers. Track brand searches, enquiries, repeat buyers and the answers customers give when asked how they found the brand, and treat likes as supporting evidence.

Social media marketing earns its budget when finance can see its link to revenue. Review these numbers monthly against sales conversations, and let the brand strategy decide what to stop posting as well as what to post.
